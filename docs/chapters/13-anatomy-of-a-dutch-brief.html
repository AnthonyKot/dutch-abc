Part V · The real documents — Chapter 13

Anatomy of a Dutch brief

Twelve chapters have taught you to read a sentence. This one is about the page the sentences are sitting on — because an official letter is not written to be read from the top, and the block printed largest is rarely the one that asks anything of you.

Before you start (ch. 12)

A Dutch letter dates itself 04-07-2026. How confident are you that this is 4 July rather than 7 April, and on what?

Answer

Confident, and from two things. The four-digit group is last, so the shape is day‑month‑year rather than year‑month‑day. And a Dutch-language letter from a Dutch sender normally uses that shape unless the document signals otherwise. That is the second level of chapter 12's hierarchy — strong conventional evidence, not an explicit statement — and a date on the page with a part above twelve would confirm it outright.

The device An official Dutch letter is assembled from blocks, and each block has a job and a conventional position: who is writing, who to, what this document is, what number to quote, what it has decided, what it costs, what you may do about it. Once you can see the blocks, you stop reading a letter and start going to the part you need. And the reason that matters is a mismatch built into the genre: prominence and obligation are not the same thing. The largest type on the page announces the letter's subject. The sentence that requires something of you is usually further down, in smaller type, under a heading.
Scope, and it is narrower than the subject invites. This chapter reads a letter's shape. It shows you where the sentence that requires something sits and how to recognise it. It does not tell you what happens if you ignore a deadline, whether you should object, or what any of it means for you — none of that is printed on the page, and this book reports only what is. Where the letter is silent, the correct output is a note saying so.

Step 1 On the page

A letter from a gemeente. Read it, and then answer two questions: what does this letter require of you, and by when?

Datum 16 april 2026 · Ons kenmerk VB-2026-00412 · Betreft aanvraag parkeervergunning

Geachte heer, mevrouw,

Hoogachtend, namens het college van burgemeester en wethouders, het hoofd Vergunningen

There is no sentence here you cannot take apart. Is toegewezen is a passive with the actor left out, which is chapter 09. Betaalt u niet op tijd, dan vervalt de toewijzing is a fronted condition with a stated consequence, which is chapters 02 and 10. Uiterlijk 15 mei 2026 is chapter 12. You have the tools for every line.

And the commonest reading of this letter is that it requires nothing. It opens by telling you that you have been granted what you asked for. The good news is in the first body line, it is unambiguous, and a reader who has spent an effortful two minutes getting that far very reasonably stops.

What the letter actually says is that you have a permit for twenty-nine days unless you pay, and the sentence carrying that is in the sixth block, under a heading, in the same type as everything else. Nothing about the page's design points at it. The letter is not being deceptive — it is being ordinary, and this is the shape ordinary official letters have.

Notice too that there are two deadlines, and they do different jobs. One is for paying. One is for disagreeing. They are counted differently, they have different consequences, and neither is a substitute for the other. A reader who takes away a single date has taken away the wrong thing.

The identification block, by function rather than by label

This is the block worth learning first, because it answers two questions at once: what am I holding, and what do I quote when I write back? Its labels are not standard. You may meet any of these, and you may meet none:

Betreft · Onderwerp · Ons kenmerk · Uw kenmerk · Uw brief van · Aanslagnummer · Zaaknummer what it concerns · subject · our reference · your reference · your letter of · assessment number · case number

Ons kenmerk and uw kenmerk are worth separating carefully, and chapter 11 already gave you what you need: ons is theirs and uw is yours. The number to quote back is the sender's — it is how they find the file. Some letters carry both, and some carry a domain-specific identifier instead of any kenmerk at all.

The labels themselves are the least well-attested thing in this chapter. Donaldson's letter appendix, printed 246, lists Uw kenmerk and referentie among the expressions of formal correspondence, and Met verwijzing naar uw brief van… — so those are sourced. Betreft and Onderwerp are not, and neither of the two real documents behind this book uses either: one is a form with no letter metadata at all, and the other identifies itself with three unlabelled lines and an Aanslagnummer. They are given here as labels you may meet, not as fields to look for — including in step 1's letter, which uses them because they are plausible and not because they are attested. Learn the block by its job.

Prominence is not obligation

This is the chapter's one idea and it is worth stating flatly. A letter's layout ranks its blocks by subject, not by what they demand of you. The headline says what the letter is about. It is set largest because that is what a subject line is for. The sentence that requires an act sits lower, in ordinary type, under a heading that names an activity rather than announcing an obligation.

Step 1 is a mild case. A tax assessment can be sharper still: it can lead with an amount coming to you, set in the largest type on the page, and put the only thing it requires below a calculation table two-thirds of the way down.

The heading that names the action is your best signal, and it is usually a verb phrase or a question. Wat u nu moet doen, Bezwaar maken, Aangifte wijzigen, Is uw rekeningnummer niet bekend? — those are section headings, not sentences, and they are the map.

A letter is not a form

Everything above describes a brief. A formulier is a different genre with a different anatomy, and mistaking one for the other is a good way to hunt for a deadline in the wrong place.

brief

Navigates by position. You learn where things sit. The metadata is top right; the action is a headed section in the lower half; the closing is last.

formulier

Navigates by numbered section. A page of toelichting comes first and carries no fields at all; it points forward by number — see 3 — rather than by place. The signature is the last numbered section.

The practical consequence: on a form, read the numbered section headings before anything else, and treat the opening explanation as an index rather than as an introduction. It will tell you which section decides what.

Step 3 Read it

The skim, in this order

  1. Identification block. What is this document, and what number do I quote when I reply? Upper right, level with your address.
  2. Date. Every relative period in the letter is counted from something, and the letter's own date is the commonest anchor.
  3. Headings. Read only the headings, top to bottom. The ones naming an activity or asking a question are where the action lives.
  4. The action passage. In those sections, look for chapter 08's obligation verbs and chapter 12's dates. Expect a passage, not a sentence — a requirement often comes with a condition, a route and a consequence spread over several lines.
  5. The objection section, if the letter decides something. A letter that only informs you usually will not have one — though its absence is weaker evidence than its presence, so do not conclude too much from a letter that lacks it.
  6. Write down actor, required action and deadline — the same three lines Part III has been filling in since chapter 08.

Two lines are worth pausing on. The consequence is stated, which by chapter 10 settles the question of whether the deadline is real — dan vervalt de toewijzing leaves nothing to infer. And the permit is not yet in force: wordt pas geldig nadat het bedrag is bijgeschreven. That is the letter's actual content, and it directly contradicts the impression its opening line creates.

You can stop here and use the procedure. Identification block, date, headings only, the action passage, the objection section, then actor / action / deadline. Everything below is practice, two limits, and where the conventions came from.

Three to try

For each, say what the document is, what it requires, and by when — and name the block each answer came from. Write it down first. One of the three requires nothing at all, and recognising that is the skill.

  1. Onderwerp: wijziging van uw premie. Per 1 januari 2027 wordt uw maandpremie € 148,50. U hoeft hiervoor niets te doen. Wilt u overstappen? Dan kunt u uw polis t/m 31 december 2026 opzeggen.
  2. Betreft: herinnering. Wij hebben uw betaling nog niet ontvangen. Wij verzoeken u het openstaande bedrag binnen 14 dagen over te maken. Bij uitblijven van betaling dragen wij de vordering over.
  3. Uw aanvraag is ontvangen op 3 maart 2026. Wij nemen binnen acht weken een beslissing. Deze termijn kan eenmaal worden verlengd. U ontvangt hierover bericht.
Show the worked answers

One. A change, with an option attached. U hoeft hiervoor niets te doen is chapter 08 doing exactly its job: hoeven plus a negative releases you. So the required action is none, and that is stated rather than inferred, which is the strongest form the answer can take. What the letter offers is an option with its own deadline — t/m 31 december 2026, and chapter 12 tells you 31 December is included. Actor: you, and only if you choose. Do not enter an option on the required-action line. The letter has two different things in it and collapsing them loses the difference.

Two. This one requires something and dresses it as a request. Wij verzoeken u … over te maken is polite in form. Chapter 11 told you tone establishes nothing, and chapter 10 supplies what does: bij uitblijven van betaling dragen wij de vordering over is a stated consequence, so the request is a requirement. Required action: pay the outstanding amount. Deadline: fourteen days — and from what is not stated on this fragment. Actor for the consequence is wij, named, which is unusual enough to notice; by chapter 09 most letters would have written it without an actor.

Three. Nothing is required, and that is the answer. This is an acknowledgement. It records a date, promises a decision within a period, warns that the period may be extended once, and says you will hear. There is no obligation verb directed at you anywhere in it. Required action: none. Deadline: none for you — the eight weeks constrain the sender, not the reader, and reading it as your deadline is the mistake this exercise is for. Actor of nemen and verlengd: the sender, stated in the first case and removed by the passive in the second.

Did it work?

  • An action with no date, or a date with no action, means the skim is not finished. Go back to the headings. One of them names a section you did not read.
  • Did you check whether the deadline is stated more than once? A letter that offers two routes — online and on paper, say — can give the same date two different meanings: send by for one and arrive by for the other. If you wrote down one deadline, check whether the letter offered you a choice of route.
  • Did you take the largest type as the point? Prominence ranks the subject, not the obligation. If your answer came from the headline alone, you have probably recorded what the letter is about rather than what it requires.
  • Can you name the number to quote when replying? If not, you have not found the identification block, and it is the one piece of the letter you will need again.
  • Are the two kinds of deadline separate on your page? A deadline for doing what the letter asks and a deadline for disagreeing with it are different acts. Neither substitutes for the other.
  • Did you distinguish required from optional? Exercise one offered a choice and exercise three required nothing. Recording either as an obligation is as wrong as missing one.

A fork you can usually resolve — and chapter 12 owed you this one

Chapter 12 left t.a.v. with two expansions and promised this chapter would settle it. Ter attentie van routes a letter to a person; ten aanzien van introduces a topic. They are different words doing different jobs and they abbreviate identically.

The block it sits in decides. In the addressee block, on its own line with a name after it, it is the routing one — for the attention of. In running prose inside the body, with a noun phrase after it, it is the topic one — with regard to. That is the chapter's whole thesis in miniature: the same string means different things in different positions, and position is information.

It resolves in nearly every real case, which is why this is a fork and not a limit. Where it would not resolve is a bare heading line carrying neither a name nor a sentence — rare enough to be worth noting and not worth planning for.

Where this procedure cannot decide

Beschikking · fragment

Datum beschikking 2 maart 2026 · Datum verzending 6 maart 2026

U kunt binnen zes weken bezwaar maken.

Three dates are in play and only two are printed. The document was decided on one day and sent on another, and the period is given with no anchor at all — binnen zes weken, counted from something the sentence does not name. Six weeks from 2 March is 13 April. Six weeks from 6 March is 17 April. Four days apart, on the only deadline that matters.

Nothing in this book closes that. It is not a vocabulary problem: every word is plain and the grammar is chapter 02's. The sentence is complete and the information is absent, which is a different failure and one no amount of reading skill repairs. Step 1's letter shows the contrast — there the period was anchored explicitly, na de datum van deze brief, and the ambiguity never arises.

Nor will the second level of chapter 12's hierarchy help, and it is worth seeing why. Knowing the letter is Dutch settles which conventions are in play — it settled the date shape at a glance. It does not settle which of the two printed dates this letter counts from, because that is fixed somewhere outside the letter and the letter has not said. Provenance selects between conventions; it does not supply facts the document withheld.

So the honest record is six weeks, from either 2 or 6 March; the letter does not say which. That is a precise statement of what you know and it names exactly what to ask. Working to the earlier of two candidate dates is a decision about risk, not a reading of the document, and this chapter's job ends at the reading. It is worth saying plainly: what the deadline does, and what follows from missing it, is not on this page either, and nothing in this book will tell you.

Step 4 In your own post

Take one official letter — a gemeente decision, an insurer, a housing association, a utility. Do not read it. Map it first: mark the blocks from step 2 in the margin, and note which ones are missing. Most letters are missing several, and which ones are absent tells you what kind of letter it is.

Then run the skim in order and time it. The identification block, the date, the headings alone, the action passage, the objection section. On a letter you have already read and understood, this should take well under a minute — and if the answer it produces differs from what you thought the letter said the first time, that difference is the whole lesson of this chapter.

Run the same tests from step 3, and the third one especially: check whether your answer came from the largest type on the page. That is the trap the layout sets, and it is set by convention rather than by intent.

Then record what stayed open, and there will be two kinds. Any period given without an anchor — binnen zes weken with no na … attached — where the letter prints more than one date it might be counting from. Write down both candidates rather than choosing. And any letter you could not classify as deciding something or merely informing you: the presence of an objection section is good evidence that it decides, and its absence is weaker evidence that it does not.

What chapter 14 will do with this

Chapter 14 introduces nothing. It takes one letter — a reconstructed Belastingdienst assessment, composed for this book — and reads it from the top with everything at once: the compounds, the bracket, a split verb, the participles, the reference chains, the pointer words, the obligation, the missing actor, the conditions, the register, the notation, and this chapter's blocks. If it needs a device no chapter has given you, that is a defect in the book rather than in the letter.

References

The conventions are Donaldson's Appendix 1, Letter writing, printed 244–247. It describes the letter you write, which makes it the mirror image of what this chapter needs and useful anyway: the parts have the same names from either side.

This chapter is where a forty-five-year-old source can be checked against a contemporary document, and it comes out mixed. That is worth setting out plainly rather than hedging, because the pattern is the useful thing.

The physical layout survived and the social language did not, which is consistent with what chapter 11 found about pronouns: the forms are stable and the conventions attached to them move. It is also a reason to trust this source on anatomy while distrusting it on courtesy.

Printed 246 also supplies the stock phrases of formal correspondence, and two of them are earlier chapters arriving in their natural habitat. Het verschuldigde bedrag — the amount owed — is chapter 06's extended attributive participle, sitting in a phrase book from 1981. And Ik sluit … hierbij in and hierbij ingesloten vindt u put chapter 07's pointer words into the enclosure line. The same page has Uw brief d.d. 15 mei, where d.d. is de dato — an abbreviation chapter 12 did not carry, because it is here rather than in the abbreviations appendix.

Two further reading facts from printed 244, both small and both real. Afz. is afzender, the sender, and marks a return address. And a Dutch surname carrying de, den or van is alphabetised under the noun rather than the preposition — which is where to look when a list appears not to contain a name you know is on it.

The block structure itself is not from Donaldson. It is taken from the two real documents behind this book — an assessment and a government form — with the invented letter in step 1 built to the same pattern. Two documents is a small sample and the chapter says so where it matters: the set of jobs is stable, the labels are not, and a letter with no identification block at all is uncommon rather than impossible.

Terug

  • A letter asks politely and states what will happen if you do not comply. Which of those two settles whether it is a requirement? (ch. 10, 11)

    The stated consequence. Tone establishes nothing — wij verzoeken u with a consequence attached is a requirement, and a formal sentence with none may be a suggestion.

  • Which obligation verb releases you, and what does it need beside it to do so? (ch. 08)

    Hoeven, with a negative — u hoeft niets te doen. Without the negative it is not doing that job, and it is not interchangeable with moeten, which a negative sends the other way.

  • A sentence says a decision will be taken and names nobody. How do you record the actor? (ch. 09)

    By its evidence level, not by a guess: stated, recoverable, probable or not stated. In a letter from an institution to you, the actor of a decision is normally recoverable — it is the sender.

  • Why does t/m 31 december settle something that tot 31 december does not? (ch. 12)

    T/m is tot en met and includes the day named — explicit notation, the top of the hierarchy. Bare tot is usually read as excluding it but is used both ways, so the reading is probable rather than stated.
